Customer experience

The score tells you how it felt.Execution tells you what happened.

Customers do not experience your org chart, your systems or your SLA definition. They experience elapsed time and repetition — both produced by execution, both already recorded, and rarely assembled into one journey.

What experience measurement shows

  • The score, the channel and the sentiment
  • The contacts a customer made
  • SLA attained against the target

What execution shows

  • The days behind the score, and where they went
  • The internal handoffs between those contacts
  • The wait the customer actually experienced

Feedback tells you where you stand. Execution tells you what to fix.

The experience gap

Every complaint arrives as a case.Every case was a path.

A customer counts from the moment they asked to the moment it was done. The enterprise counts in the segments each team owns, and each segment can hit its target while the whole takes weeks. This is why a process can be inside SLA at every step and still produce a customer who has been waiting since the start of the month — and why the fix is almost never in the team that received the complaint.

From feeling to fix

Journey, cause, consequence

Feedback identifies which customers had a poor experience. These three steps identify what produced it and what it is costing — which is the part that turns a CX programme into an operational change.

01 · Journey

Reconstruct what the customer had

Assemble the whole journey end to end, across every system that touched it, on the customer's clock rather than each team's.

02 · Cause

Locate what produced it

Where did the time and the friction come from?

What the customer feels

Four things execution produces, none of them on a dashboard

Experience is rarely damaged by a single failure. It is damaged by ordinary operational behaviour that no individual team owns and no individual system reports.

Waiting

Time when nothing is happening

The dead hours and days between touches. Usually the largest part of what a customer experienced, and the part least likely to appear in a cycle-time report.

Repetition

Asked again for what was given

Documents re-requested, details re-confirmed, the same question from a second team. The single most reliable predictor of a poor score.

Handoff

How many teams the case crossed

Each transfer adds queue time, context loss and another chance to ask for something already provided. The customer sees none of them and feels all of them.

Silence

Cases where nothing moved

Requests that stalled without breaching anything, because no clock was running on the gap. These are the ones customers chase, then abandon.

Journeys that cross teams before they reach an answer
